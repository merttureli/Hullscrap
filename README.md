# Hullscrap

A top-down 2D spaceship game. Start with a ready-made ship (or an empty hull), build walls, rooms and systems on a grid, run power to them, crew the ship, fly it, dock and fight. The plan is in [`docs/PLAN.md`](docs/PLAN.md).

## Play the beta (no install)
Every push to `main` builds the game for **Windows, Linux and macOS** on GitHub Actions (the **beta builds** workflow).
1. On GitHub, open **Actions → beta builds**, then the latest green run.
2. Under **Artifacts**, download **Hullscrap-Windows** (or Linux / macOS) and unzip it.
3. Run **Hullscrap.exe** (Linux: `Hullscrap.x86_64`; macOS: unzip the app, then right-click → Open the first time, because it isn't signed).
Saves go to Godot's user folder (on Windows: `%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Hullscrap`).

## Play from the source (with the Godot editor)
1. Install **Godot 4.6.3** (standard build, not .NET) from https://godotengine.org/download/archive/4.6.3-stable/.
2. Open Godot, choose **Import**, and pick this folder's `project.godot`.
3. Press **F5** to play. The game opens on **Choose your ship** (see [Starter ships](#starter-ships)): press **1**, **2** or **3**, then **B** for play mode and **K** to fight the test pirate (**Shift+K**: the Rustfang rammer). **M** opens the map of the Drift (the campaign).

Every starter is a working ship: closed rooms joined by 2-wide doors, every part on a cable with supply above demand, a helm and engines that fly (engines on the rear wall, guns through the bow, each in two halves), an oxygen generator, a fabricator alone in its workshop, crew beds, spare parts and some credits in the cargo (`"credits": N`; credits are just a cargo count for now). ## Crew, fire and repairs
Every starter comes with four people: your soldier, Ada, and two more NPCs (Rook and Juno; names and suit colours come from `data/crew.json`) who start on the beds. The pirate has three enemy crew. Everyone has 100 hp.

**Fire** (`scripts/sim/fire.gd`, `ship.fires`: cell -> intensity 0 to 1, saved with the ship). A fire burns on a floor cell (a cell under a system too; doors and walls are not floor, so a fire stays in its room).
- Each second: while the room's air is above 20% the intensity grows by 0.15, below it the fire decays by 0.3 and goes out at 0; each burning cell has a 10% chance to light a random neighbouring floor cell and uses 0.05 of the room's oxygen; a system on a burning cell loses 0.5 hp (at 0 it is disabled, not destroyed).
- Fires start when a system overheats (20%) or malfunctions (30%), on the system's first cell, and when a hit in a fight ignites (`fire_chance`), on the cell that was hit.
- Drawn from `art/fx/fire` (a flame per burning cell, with its glow), or as flickering orange and red blobs when that art is missing (`ShipView.draw_fire()`), in the interior and on the ships in a fight.

**Health.** Standing in a burning cell costs 10 hp a second, a room below 15% air 5. At 0 hp a crew member is **down**: they don't move, and a crewmate revives them (3 s of work, back with 20 hp) or a powered medbay does (15 hp a second, times the medbay's `stats.heal`). A bed heals 5 hp a second. A small health bar hangs over anyone hurt. **Bleed out:** a downed crew member dies after 45 s (`bleed_out_s` in `data/crew.json`) unless a crewmate is working a revive on them or they lie in a room with a powered, working medbay (the timer stops, then starts over once they are up); a red ring over them empties as the time runs out. Your soldier never dies, he just stays down; enemy crews follow the same rule. The dead stay dead (`scripts/crew/mortality.gd`).

**Jobs** (`scripts/crew/jobs.gd`, numbers in `data/crew.json`). Every second the ship's job board is rebuilt from its state and idle NPCs take the highest-priority job nobody is on (two may fight the same fire), the nearest first by walking distance (only the few nearest get a path search). They keep a job until it is done or one at least 30 points higher shows up. Your soldier is never given a job.

| Priority | Job | Work |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | Extinguish a burning cell | 0.5 intensity per second per crew member; two stack |
| 95 | Revive a downed crew member | 3 s |
| 90 | Repair a breach | 6 s x the repair multiplier of the wall's line; the wall comes back and the room seals |
| 80 (90 for a weapon, engine or shield in a fight) | Repair a malfunctioning or disabled system, or a hull mount's damaged outside half (from the inside, while the wall between the halves is whole; the outside half heals first) | 5 s x the repair multiplier of the system's line (scrap 0.5, standard 1, neon 1.5). An overheated system just needs time |
| 70 | Repair a cut conveyor belt cell (damage next to a belt cuts it; see Ammo racks and belts) | 2 s a cell |
| 50 | Man a station: the helm, and weapons, shields or engines whose data says `manned: true` | stands next to it |
| 40 | Restock an ammo rack below half full from the cargo | 5 units a second at normal speed (x the crew member's `restock` work_mult: a Grundl x2); once started they fill it to the top |
| 30 | Clear a wreck: the debris of a part that was shot to pieces | 4 s; gives back half its recipe materials, rounded down |
| 10 | Rest, when hurt: a powered medbay, else a bed | heals |

A manned helm adds 5% evasion in a fight (`Jobs.evasion_bonus(ship)`; the turrets' hit roll reads it) and a manned weapon cools down 10% faster (wired into `Arena.fire`). `ship.manned` maps each manned system's uid to the crew member at it.

The HUD (bottom left) has one line, "Crew: Ada repairing Laser Mk1, Rook extinguishing, Juno at helm, ...", and the chronicle logs "Fire in engine room", "Fire out", "Breach sealed", "Kickjet repaired", "Rook is down", "Rook is back on their feet".

## Combat
In play mode (**B**) press **K** to meet the test pirate (`data/ships/pirate_raider.json`, 1200 px ahead). The camera zooms out to frame both ships, the interior is put away and the crew are drawn on the hulls: they keep working (fires, breaches, repairs, stations) on both ships. Power, heat, oxygen, fires and malfunctions keep running on both ships.

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| W / S | Throttle forward / reverse |
| A / D | Strafe left / right on the engines mounted on the side walls (no side engines, no strafe). With `"controls": {"steer": "keys"}` in `data/flight.json` they turn the ship instead |
| Mouse | Steer: the ship turns toward the cursor at its turn rate, and **fixed guns aim where the ship points** (their barrels follow the cursor inside a few degrees of gimbal). A faint boresight line and cone shows where each fixed gun points; a turret shows its arc as a wedge and a ring, and tracks the enemy by itself |
| T | Turrets autofire on / off (they fire when loaded and on target; the weapon bar shows each turret's hit chance and AUTO or HOLD) |
| Left mouse (hold) | Fire group 1: lasers, bolts, beams |
| Right mouse (hold) | Fire group 2: missiles, flak |
| 1 - 9 | Fire that weapon (the slot numbers on the weapon bar) |
| Shift | Engine ability (a Kickjet's Sputter Boost...) |
| Space | Shield ability: Overcharge gives one extra layer for 6 s, then 15 s of rest |
| Q | Utility ability (the Prism Core's Overdrive...). In play mode, out of a fight, **hold Q** to run the tractor beams (see Salvage while you drift) |
| Arrow keys | Shield balance, one step (half a weight) toward a side: **Up** the bow, **Down** the stern, **Left** port, **Right** starboard (port and starboard need 4+ sectors; the step is taken from the opposite sector first, then evenly from the others; no step past 0 or 2) |
| End | Shield balance back to even |
| Esc | Leave, once the pirate is wrecked, captured, spared, gone or more than 4000 px away |
| Y (fight) | Accept a surrender the enemy is offering (keep firing to refuse) |
| Y (map, no fuel) | Ma tows you home to Gantry Rusthold: debt +150, 2 fuel (so a run never gets stuck) |
| J | Spool the jump drive: after 10 s you escape the fight from any range (a fled fight gives no loot). The spool follows the same rule as an enemy's (`Morale.spool_hold`): it holds, and the banner says "Jump drive held: engines out" (or "helm down", "no crew"), while all your engines are out, your helm is down or all your crew are down; it goes on when one is back |
| R | After your ship is wrecked: back to build mode with the empty starter hull reloaded (B, then N, picks a ship again) |

**Surrender, flight and capture** (`scripts/combat/morale.gd`, numbers and styles in `data/morale.json`). A fight has more endings than a wrecked hull, so what you shoot at matters. A losing enemy **breaks** once per fight: when its hull is at 35% or under, or when none of its weapons has been able to fire for 5 s. It then rolls its style (a design's `"morale"` entry in its `ai`: `rustfang`, `ram`, `concord`, `hollow` or the `default`) for one of three things. **Surrender**: it holds fire (guns and turrets) and cuts its throttle, and hails an offer (credits, fuel or missiles, now and then one of its parts) for 12 s. Press **Y** to take it and the fight ends *spared*; hit it (or let the time run out) and it fights to the end, and morale never triggers again. Your turrets hold fire while an offer stands, so they do not refuse it by accident, and go back to how they were if it is refused; shots already in flight when it broke do not count as a refusal. **Flee**: it turns its stern to you and burns while its jump drive charges for 15 s (a red JUMP bar under its schematic; its guns still fire when they bear). The charge holds while every engine of it is out (disabled, offline or unpowered), while its helm is down (none working and powered), or while every crew member aboard is down, and the bar says which ("JUMP held: engines out", "helm down" or "no crew"), so hit the engines, the nozzles, the reactor or the helm room. Whether somebody happens to stand at the helm does not count. When it fills, the ship is gone with its loot (a jump flash and "They got away"). **Captured**: if its crew was aboard and every member is down (or none is left) for 3 s, its guns and helm stop and the ship is yours with the hull whole. `state()` is `won`, `captured`, `spared`, `escaped`, `fighting` or `lost` (lost wins over everything); the result the view hands the map is `{state, enemy, hull, hull_max, threat}` with `state` one of `won`, `captured`, `spared`, `escaped`, `fled` (you left) or `lost`, and `offer: {item: count}` when spared.

**The shield bubble** (`scripts/combat/shield_shape.gd`, `shields.gd`, `shield_balance.gd`; design in `docs/CONTENT.md` 4). It is the convex hull of the ship's solid cells (walls, doors, parts and the outside halves of mounts), inflated 1.5 cells with rounded corners, so it hugs a long, wedge-shaped or lumpy ship and never cuts into the hull; it is rebuilt when the hull changes and shots test against that outline. The best powered generator sets the **sector count** (`stats.sectors`: 1 for the Mk1 and scrap, 2 for the Mk2, 4 for the Neon Lattice, 8 later), bow first and clockwise. Each sector has a **weight** (steps of 0.5, 0 to 2, adding up to the sector count) and holds `layers x weight` layers: with 2 layers on bow and stern, even is 2 and 2, the **Bow** preset 4 and 0. A hit uses a layer of the sector it enters and each sector recharges its own (2 s a layer). **Shifting takes time**: the sector that loses weight drops to its new capacity at once, the one that gains it fills at the recharge rate. Firing opens only the sector the shot leaves through. A changed sector count (a generator lost or repaired) starts from even weights again; Overcharge adds a layer to every sector in proportion to its weight. The bubble is drawn along its outline, each sector as bright and thick as its layers, dashed at 0, shimmering while it fills and flashing when hit; the small diagram left of the weapon bar shows each sector's layers (dark while open). The pilot AI with 2+ sectors shifts toward the side facing you, one step every `1.5 - ai.aim_skill` seconds (default skill 0.6). The balance you set in the Shields tab is kept in the ship's save (`shield_balance`, ship save version 4; older saves fight even) and is how every fight starts.

The panels at the top are the schematics (yours left, the enemy's right): rooms, walls, breaches in red, systems coloured by state (green ok, yellow damaged, red disabled, orange overheated, purple jammed, grey unpowered), a hull bar and the shield pips. **Firing opens your own shield**: the sector of the bubble the shot leaves through drops for 0.3 s (laser), 0.4 s (bolt), 0.6 s (flak) or the whole beam, while shots pass it and use no layer; the schematic shows "OPEN" and the sector ring goes dark. Missiles never drop it. Open doors let shots through; closed doors block them. The weapon bar shows each weapon's key, group, cooldown ring and state (ready, cooling, no power, jammed, no ammo, disabled, offline, out of arc for a turret), and under each turret its hit chance against the enemy. **Enemy intent** (`scripts/combat/intent.gd`, numbers in `data/combat_ui.json`): a pulsing red reticle sits on the part of your ship the enemy pilot is aiming at (held at least 0.5 s so it does not flicker), "Targeting: Weapons" shows under your schematic, and each heavy enemy weapon (damage 3+, a missile, a beam or ion, with a cooldown of at least 3 s) that will be ready within 1.5 s adds a row such as "Artemis in 1.2 s" (red under 0.5 s), so you can shift the shield balance or turn your armour before the hit.

**Hull mounts, fixed guns and turrets** (`docs/CONTENT.md` 4f, `scripts/ship/mounts.gd`). A weapon or engine is two halves joined through a wall of any thickness: the **outside half** (barrel or nozzles) sits in space against the hull and takes the hits you can see, with its own hp (75% of the part's), and is only ever **disabled**, never destroyed: shots and rams then go on into the wall behind it. The **inside half** is on the floor behind the wall, where crew man, wire and repair it. If any wall cell between the halves is breached the mount is **offline** until crew patch it. A direct hit on an interior part that is already disabled **wrecks** it (both halves of a mount): it is gone, with debris on its cells that a crew job (`clear_wreck`, 4 s) clears for half its recipe materials, rounded down (nothing for a unique). Fire, heat, ion and malfunctions never wreck. Most guns are **fixed** and fire along the mount's outward direction (up at rotation 0); **turrets** (Rattle Burst, Siren Ion, Leech Laser, Concord Point Turret) track within their arc and roll a hit (accuracy less the target's evasion, 25% at full speed, 5% more with a manned helm) for 60% of the damage. An engine pushes opposite its nozzle: rear engines (rotation 2) drive the ship, side engines strafe it.

**Loot and salvage** (`scripts/space/salvage.gd`, `Campaign.fight_done`). A win pays credits (times the tier) and then opens a **salvage pick** in the event panel: cut out one of 3 of the enemy's parts that survived the fight (4 when her crew was all down and she was captured whole), scan the best one whose blueprint you do not know (the way blueprints come in), or strip her for scrap (20 + 6 per part, times the tier; a captured ship pays that scrap and 1.5x the credits at once instead). Parts you wrecked, beds and helms are not on the list. A ship that is spared pays its offer, one that escaped pays nothing (`data/drift.json` `loot`). **A dangerous enemy pays better** (Slay the Spire's elites): the pick offers 1 more part against an enemy of 4 skulls of threat and 2 more against 5 (`loot.threat_picks`; the result of the fight carries `threat`, `Campaign.fight_done` hands it to `Salvage.event_for`), and the page adds "She was dangerous. There's more worth cutting." The Hollow rule still comes first: a ghost ship offers only the strip row.

**Threat you can read** (`scripts/space/campaign.gd` `foe_for`, `scripts/ui/skulls.gd`). The enemy of a fight is rolled before you choose it: `Campaign.foe_for(node, kind)` rolls a design or a generated ship from a seed made of the run seed, the region seed, the node and the kind (never the campaign rng), with the budget of the moment, and keeps it in the save (`foes`, campaign save version 6), so `fight_path("tier", ...)` hands out the foe the event page rated. A choice whose outcomes fight ends with a tag, `[Threat 4/5]`, the skulls of that enemy against your ship (`Shipgen.threat`): dim for 1-2, amber for 3, red for 4-5, and `[may fight: Threat 4/5]` when only some of its outcomes fight. A fight opens with a 3 s banner ("Rustfang Cutter: Threat 4/5") and the enemy's schematic carries its skulls in the title bar.

**Ramming and ricochets** (`scripts/combat/impact_fx.gd`). When two ships collide hard enough to hurt, sparks and debris fly from the contact points (`fx/sparks`, `fx/explosion`), the screen shakes by the impact energy, a banner and the chronicle say "Rammed the pirate: 14 hull damage (took 6)", and the walls take their damage as usual. A shot that glances off a hull shows a small spark at the bounce point.

**Hazards** (`scripts/combat/hazards.gd`, numbers and texts in `data/hazards.json`, `docs/MECHANICS_REVIEW.md` P8). Some places change *how* you fight. A region's file lists the hazards it may have (`hazards`: `ion_storm`, `star_heat`, `gas_cloud`, `radiation`, `debris_surge`); each node of that region may roll one, by node type (empty 50%, anomaly 60%, asteroid field 40%, wreck field 30%, other nodes 20%; stations, the exit and dead gates never), evenly among the region's list, with a direction in 45 degree steps, once per node from the region seed (`Campaign.hazard_at(id)`, the same every time). The map shows a small badge on a known node with a hazard, and its tooltip names it, says what it does and where the star or the current is. A fight at that node runs with `arena.hazard = {id, dir}` (the map sets it when it starts the fight) and the fight HUD shows its line under the hint ("ION STORM: shields recharge at half speed"), with an arrow toward the star or the current. **Ion storm**: shield layers come back at half speed (the 3 s ion lock is not slowed): shield bricks can be cracked, so press the attack. **Star heat**: every 25 s a flare lights up to 3 fires on each ship, on floor cells beside the outer walls that face the star; the HUD says "Flare in 3" 3 s before, so turn your armour, guns and empty rooms toward the star and keep the reactor away from it. **Gas cloud**: turret accuracy -0.2 and the enemy pilot's aim error x1.5, so fixed guns and good flying beat turrets. **Radiation**: while a ship has no shield layer up (or its shields are ion-locked), every crew member aboard without the `suit` trait loses 0.5 hp/s: keep the shields up, and an ion hit becomes a crew emergency that pairs with bleed out. **Debris surge**: every 20 s, 4 kinetic hits of 1 damage land on random outer wall cells that face the current, on each ship, through the normal damage rules (resist, slopes, breaches), with a "Hits in 3" warning: armour the upstream side. A wall "faces" the star or the current when a free face of it that looks out into space points within 60 degrees of it. The timers run on the arena tick (the first flare or surge comes `every_s` into the fight); a surge hit is not taken for an attack on a surrendering enemy; with no hazard no number changes and nothing is drawn from the arena's rng.

Characters are big: a crew body is 2x2 cells (drawn about 3x3), so doors are a piece of exactly 2 cells in a row with a wall cell at each end (wall, door, door, wall), and a corridor needs 2 cells. Walls stay 1 cell thick. A door costs per cell; removing or replacing either cell takes the whole piece, and a wall holding a door can't be removed. Systems are items from the cargo: placing one uses an item up, removing it gives the item back. A system needs floor under every cell, and that floor can't be removed or built over while it stands there. Cables cost copper per cell (`data/build.json`) and may run under anything. A system only works when a cable connects it to a reactor; an unpowered one is drawn dimmed with a red mark.

## Ammo racks and belts
(`docs/CONTENT.md` 4f; logic in `scripts/sim/ammo.gd` and `scripts/sim/belts.gd`, numbers in `data/ammo.json`, `data/crew.json` and the parts' `stats` in `data/components.json`.) A weapon with an `ammo` item (missiles, scrap) draws it like this:
- **A ship with no ammo rack at all** draws from the cargo, as it always did (enemy designs, old saves). The first **Ammo Rack** (2x2, on the Utility tab: 14 scrap and 5 copper, a starting blueprint) switches the ship's placed guns to racks.
- **With racks**, a gun draws from the racks **in the same room as its inside half** (a door makes a new room), or from the nearest rack on its **belt network** (below). The cargo is only the stock the crew restock the racks from. A rack holds 20 units of **one** ammo item (the Grundl Cargo Deck doubles the racks in its room), shows its fill level on its art with the count, and takes nothing while disabled. Refusals, shown on the weapon bar: "no ammo rack in reach" (NO RACK), "rack empty" (RACK EMPTY), "belt stopped" (the ammo is on a belt but no working drive moves it), and "feeding" (the unit is on its way along the belt: the reload waits for it). With no racks the old "no ammo" stays.
- **Restock** (job, priority 40): the crew carry ammo from the cargo to any rack below 50%: 1 s per 5 units, walking to it, times their `restock` work_mult (Grundl x2, which is what cancels the Slug Mortar's and the Barge Flak's appetite). An empty rack is stocked with what the guns in its reach spend. You can do it by hand: stand next to the rack and hold E.
- **A wrecked rack holding explosive ammo (missiles; `explosive: true` in `data/items.json`) explodes**: 2 damage per unit, as one explosive hit on every part in its room and every wall and door around the room. A full magazine of missiles will open the room to space: a big central magazine is efficient, and a liability.
- **Belt drives** (2x2, 1 power; found or traded, not a starting blueprint; each also has a recipe you can learn by scanning one): the **Clan Chain Drive** (Scrap, 3 cells/s, one unit every 0.8 s; like every Scrap part it malfunctions now and then and the belt stops until a crew member repairs ("kicks") it), the **Concord Autoloader** (Standard, 5 cells/s, 0.5 s) and the **Lumen Mag-Rail** (Neon, 8 cells/s, 0.3 s). An ion hit shuts any drive down for a few seconds (the general ion rule: Neon x2), and the belt stops with it. Rusthold, Meridian and Prism sell them, and a wreck-field event in each of their regions (pack `ammo_1`) can hand one over.
- **Belts** are a floor overlay laid in build mode (the Belt card on the Structure tab, 1 scrap a cell, floor and door cells only, never under a system). It unlocks with your first drive (the belt blueprint is learned for good). Orthogonally joined cells are a **network**; a rack, gun or drive is on it when a belt cell touches its edge. A network moves only with a powered, working drive on it (several add feed rate, the speed is the best drive's). A gun that wants ammo (it keeps two shots' worth, loaded and riding) pulls a unit from the nearest rack on its network; the unit **rides the belt visibly** and arrives after path length / speed. Damage next to a belt cell (a hit or a blast beside it) **cuts** it, splitting the network, until a crew member repairs it (job, priority 70, 2 s); a belt on a breached cell is cut until the breach is sealed. A unit riding a belt that gets cut goes back in its rack. Nothing riding is lost on a save.
- **Saves**: ship version 5 (the optional `belts` layer: cells, cut cells, the learned blueprint) and components version 4 (a rack's `ammo` and `n`, a gun's `loaded`); older saves load with no belts and empty racks.

## Salvage while you drift
In **play mode** (B) debris drifts past the ship on a current (`scripts/space/drift_debris.gd`, drawn by `scripts/view/debris_view.gd`, every number in `data/debris.json`): scrap chunks, copper coils, polymer barrels, ice chunks, sealed crates and now and then a fuel canister, about 40 px/s, the way the HUD line says ("current: south"). The stars drift with it. It stops in build mode and in a fight. **Salvage intakes** are hull mounts like guns and engines (the outside half is the open mouth, the inside half a hopper): place the **Net Mouth** Ma Gantry gives you (or fab one for 20 scrap) on an outer wall in build mode, facing upstream, and go back to play mode. Debris that touches the mouth, or passes within its pull radius (1 cell for the Net Mouth, 2 for the **Salvage Intake Mk1**, which needs 1 power: 30 scrap and 10 copper at a fabricator, or 120 credits at Meridian), is drawn in and lands in your cargo with a "+3 scrap" pop; an ice chunk refills the air by 5%, a crate is a roll (scrap, copper or a missile). Debris that hits plain hull bounces off. The HUD shows what came in "this trip". Where you put intakes decides your income: sectors have a current (20-60 px/s) and the debris streams past the ship, so a mouth facing upstream catches what a side mouth only grazes.

**The Raft parts** (Utility tab; `data/components.json`, bought at Meridian and fabbed from the Mk1 blueprints you find; `tests/test_sector3.gd`). **Salvage Intake Mk1 / Mk2 / Mk3**: pull radius 3 / 4 / 5 cells, power -1 / -1 / -2. **Radar Mk1 / Mk2 / Mk3** are hull mounts too (an inside console and an outside dish that turns, power -1 / -2 / -3): 6,000 / 10,000 / 16,000 px, the helm's own Mk0 reaching 3,000. Mk1 shows "contact" and a bearing; **Mk2 and Mk3 name what they see** (a passing ship by its kind) **and rate a pirate's or patrol's threat in 1-5 skulls** under its marker, the same rating the fight would show (`Radar.threat`, `Shipgen.threat_of`); **Mk3 also finds hidden contacts** within its range (the others only within 1,500 px). A dish shot off (the outside half), a breached wall between the halves or a console off the power blinds that radar. **Tractor Beam Mk1 / Mk2** (a turret mount, power -2 / -3): **hold Q** and every working beam hauls the debris within 300 / 450 px toward the nearest working intake, faster than an intake pulls (`scripts/space/tractor.gd`); a pale beam is drawn to each piece it holds and the emitter turns to it. Q is the utility ability in a fight, so the beams run in play mode only. Income, measured in `test_sector3.gd`: one Mk1 intake facing the current, drifting idle, brings about 15-25 scrap a minute plus a little copper and polymer; a Mk3 chased through the debris about double, and the tractor adds to that. Big hauls are the points of interest. **Quest sites** (`quest` POIs, a beacon each) are made only in a region that has a quest event: the ten homeland tech quests (`data/packs/regions_1`) put one in roughly a third to a half of that region's sectors.

## The Hollow tide
The Drift has a clock besides Ma's debt (`scripts/space/tide.gd`, numbers in `data/tide.json`, design in `docs/MECHANICS_REVIEW.md` P1). Every region has a front that sweeps over its map from where you arrive towards the exit beacon: every jump, wait for a trader and tow moves it one step (it holds still for the first 3 steps, then takes 16 steps to cross in the first region, 14 in the second, down to 10 in the eighth; a new region starts a new tide), and **lingering in one place pushes it too**: 4 minutes of play mode with the map shut (build mode and fights don't count) is one step, and arriving somewhere starts the count again. Places behind the front are **Hollow**: arriving there plays a Hollow event (`type: hollow`, the `mech_tide` pack) instead of the place's own, fights send ghost ships, and there is no trade. A fight at a Hollow place pays almost nothing: no credits, no part to cut out or scan, and a surrender offer is refused; the only salvage row is stripping her for half the usual scrap, so Hollow places cannot be farmed. **Stations and the region's exit hold.** On the map, Hollow places are dead violet-grey with a broken ring under a faint band that ends at the front; hovering a place says "The tide reaches here in 2 jumps" (or "Hollow: ghost ships, no trade", or "Holds against the tide"), and the status bar shows "Tide: 5 jumps / 2:41" (jumps until it reaches your place, time until it rises by itself). In play mode a line at the top right says "Hollow tide: rises in 2:41", amber under a minute and red under 15 seconds, and the chronicle and a toast tell you "The tide rises in 60 s", "The Hollow tide rises." and "The Hollow takes 2 more places." So "one more beacon?" is a real gamble, and sitting in a rich field to farm it costs you the way home.

## Exit wardens
Every region's exit beacon is held by a **warden**, a strong generated ship you know about before you choose the region (`scripts/space/wardens.gd`, numbers and hints in `data/wardens.json`, design in `docs/MECHANICS_REVIEW.md` P7). The guarding faction is the region's `faction` when it is the Clans, the Concord, the Lumen, the Rustfangs or the Hollow, else its `enemies` faction. The warden is built for the budget you will meet at the exit (about 8 jumps into the region), on a medium hull in stages 1 and 2 and a large one after, times 1.0 in stage 1 up to 1.5 in stage 8, and it is rolled from the run seed, the stage and the region, so the region card tells the truth. **On the region choice** each card ends with "Exit warden: Concord Bulwark (Threat 4/5)" (the threat is rated against your ship as it is now: dim at 1-2, amber at 3, red at 4-5) and a one-line hint at what beats it, drawn from what that faction really buys. **At the exit beacon** arriving opens the warden's event instead of the region choice: pay the toll (credits x the stage; the Hollow asks nothing and the Rustfangs never wave you through), a blue "[Good standing]" row that waves you through for free when your reputation with the faction is high enough, "Break through" (a fight against the warden's ship: a win, a surrender or a capture pays 50 credits x the stage and costs some reputation with the Clans, the Concord or the Lumen; an escape also clears the lane), or turn back (it stays; press Enter on the beacon to face it again). The region choice only opens once the warden is cleared, and the beacon's tooltip on the map names the warden and its threat. The ending region has no exit and no warden. Saves are version 6 (the warden; an older save rolls it on load).

## Front door
The game opens on the **main menu** (`scripts/ui/main_menu.gd`, `scripts/ui/front_door.gd`): **Continue** (only when a run is saved with Ctrl+S or "Save and quit to menu": it loads the ship and the campaign), **New run** (the ship picker; Esc there goes back to the menu), **How to play**, **Quit**. The corner shows `Beta 1 (<git hash>)` from `version.txt` (written at export, `dev` without it) and the last five runs from `user://profile.json`. **Esc** in the game opens the pause menu (Resume, How to play, Save and quit to menu, Quit; the game is paused). **F1** opens **How to play** anywhere (`scripts/ui/help_panel.gd`): one screen of keys in columns, with a First run box. In the first run only, three hint toasts appear (after the ship is picked, on the first map, on the first fight). When a campaign fight is lost, **YOUR SHIP IS SCRAP** shows the ship, jumps, credits, debt, crew alive, events seen and fights won (`scripts/ui/game_over.gd`), the run goes into `user://profile.json` and the saves are deleted: death is final. `tests/test_front_door.gd` checks the menu, the help text against the keys of the input handlers, the profile and the end screen's numbers; when you add or change a key, change `HelpPanel.COLUMNS` and the list in that test.

## Art
The game draws pixel art from `art/` and falls back to flat code-drawn placeholders for anything missing (`scripts/view/art.gd` loads and caches it; no editor import is needed). What is live:
- **Systems** (`art/components`): the sprite at the part's footprint, turned with it, animated by state (on, off, weapon charge and fire, damaged, disabled, malfunction flicker, overheated pulse) with a glow layer; the heat bar, hp pips and marks stay on top. A part with no art borrows the next lower tier's (Engine Mk2 shows the Mk1 sprite until it has its own).
- **Crew** (`art/crew`): layered top-down sprites, legs to the way they walk, torso, arms, item and head to the aim, the outfit tinted with each crew member's suit colour, a 1 px shadow.
- **Structure and effects** (`art/tiles`, `art/fx`, `art/projectiles`): autotiled walls, floors, doors that open, breaches, powered cables, fire, smoke, sparks, explosions, muzzle flashes, shield ripples, shots and beams.
- **Shaped walls and the hull skin** (`art/tiles/*_shapes`, `hull_skin*`): slopes and curves are drawn from the shaped sheets (a frame per orientation and neighbour mask; a neighbour joins a wall when it covers 60% of the shared edge), ram plating from `walls_ram*` with no tint, and the open part of a shaped wall shows floor when it looks into the ship, space otherwise. A ship in a fight also gets the exterior skin over every wall that faces space (panel variants and a few vents, antennas, hatches and lamps by position, tinted by the line most of those walls belong to; neon adds an edge glow; ram plating keeps its hazard tape). The interior and build views never show it.

The format and how to add art are in [`art/README.md`](art/README.md); every file's source is in [`ASSETS.md`](ASSETS.md). The default texture filter is nearest.
